# Ten Concepts

Sajedah Andalsi — IXD 415, Fall 2026.

Ten interaction ideas for a webcam-and-projector piece, written out before committing to one.
Numbers 1–8 came out of sketchbook brainstorming; 9 and 10 came later, aiming for something with a
stranger idea underneath it. Number 7 is the one that got built.

---

## 1. This or That

Two options float above the viewer's head. They shake their head left or right to choose, and the
chosen option drops toward them while the other fades out. A whole branching set of questions could
chain off each answer, so the piece becomes a conversation that only uses one gesture.

## 2. Catch

Food falls from the top of the frame and the viewer moves to catch it in their mouth. Face tracking
finds the mouth; a catch lands with a sound and a score, a miss splatters. The affordance explains
itself — nobody needs to be told what an open mouth under falling food means.

## 3. Mind Reader

The piece reads the expression on the viewer's face and puts a thought bubble above their head. A
frown gets *"I should have eaten before this,"* a raised eyebrow gets something smug. The joke is
that it's confidently wrong about you, and the viewer keeps making faces to see what it guesses next.

## 4. Dress Up

A closet of clothing hangs along the edge of the frame. The viewer drags pieces onto their own body
with their hands, and each piece sticks to the right part of them and follows as they move. Layering
is allowed, so the fun is in the bad combinations.

## 5. Put Yourself Somewhere Else

The webcam separates the viewer from their background and the projector puts a different place behind
them — a beach, a street, a room. They're standing *in* the scene rather than in front of it, and
moving around the space changes where in the scene they are.

## 6. Seasons

The viewer slides their hand across and the world behind them moves through summer, fall, winter,
spring. The scrub is continuous rather than stepped, so they can stop halfway and hold a half-turned
autumn — the in-between states are the point.

## 7. Gem

A crystal floats in front of the viewer and answers to their hands. Pinch to grab and turn it, sweep
to spin it, close your fist and it shatters into particles, open your hand and it pulls itself back
together. Rotation carries momentum, so a flick keeps it turning and it settles on its own.

**This is the one that got built** — see `BUILDLOG.md` and `docs/crystal-handtracking-plan.md`.

## 8. Rainbow

Movement is the only light source. The more the viewer moves, the more colour floods the screen;
hold still and everything drains to black. Stillness is punished, which makes the rule obvious within
a second or two of walking up.

---

## 9. The Shy Mirror

The piece shows the viewer's reflection, but only while they aren't looking at it. Turn your face
toward the screen and your image dissolves into particles; look away, and out of the corner of your
eye you can see yourself resolve again.

Head yaw from face landmarks drives the dissolve that's already built, so mechanically it's a small
change to work that exists. What makes it interesting is that it can't be experienced directly, only
sideways — it turns a mirror into something that avoids you, and the viewer ends up performing
disinterest to get a look at themselves.

## 10. Everyone Who Came Before

The piece keeps a silhouette of every person who has stood in front of it. New arrivals join a crowd
of faint shapes left by everyone earlier in the day, and the longer it runs the more crowded the wall
gets.

Technically it's the cheapest thing on this list — a feedback loop that never fully decays — but the
meaning accumulates on its own: the first person of the day is alone, and the last person is standing
in a room full of the people they missed.
